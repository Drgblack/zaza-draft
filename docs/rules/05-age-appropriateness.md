# Zaza Draft - Age Appropriateness Rules
## Grade-Level Appropriate Language & Content

**Version:** 3.0  
**Grade Levels:** K-12

---

## 🎯 PRINCIPLE

Language complexity, vocabulary, and content must match the developmental stage of the student being discussed.

---

## 📊 GRADE-LEVEL LANGUAGE GUIDE

### K-2 (Ages 5-7)
**Vocabulary:** Simple, concrete words
**Sentence Length:** Short (8-12 words)
**Concepts:** Observable behaviors only
**Examples:**
✅ "Jamie shares toys with friends"
✅ "Needs help staying in seat"
❌ "Demonstrates socio-emotional challenges"

### 3-5 (Ages 8-10)
**Vocabulary:** Clear, age-appropriate
**Sentence Length:** Medium (10-15 words)
**Concepts:** Concrete with some abstract
**Examples:**
✅ "Alex works well in groups"
✅ "Could improve time management"
❌ "Exhibits metacognitive deficits"

### 6-8 (Ages 11-13)
**Vocabulary:** Grade 6-8 reading level
**Sentence Length:** Mixed (10-18 words)
**Concepts:** Mix of concrete and abstract
**Examples:**
✅ "Taylor shows leadership potential"
✅ "Would benefit from organizational skills"
❌ "Demonstrates executive function disorders"

### 9-12 (Ages 14-18)
**Vocabulary:** More sophisticated
**Sentence Length:** Varied (12-20 words)
**Concepts:** Abstract thinking appropriate
**Examples:**
✅ "Jordan demonstrates critical thinking"
✅ "College readiness requires improved focus"
❌ "Manifests attention deficit pathology"

---

## 🚫 INAPPROPRIATE FOR ALL AGES

❌ Clinical/diagnostic language
❌ Psychological jargon
❌ Medical terminology
❌ Adult relationship terms
❌ Overly complex vocabulary

---

## ✅ UNIVERSAL GUIDELINES

1. **Readability:** Target Grade 6-8 for all parent communications
2. **Clarity:** Short sentences, plain English
3. **Specificity:** Observable behaviors, not interpretations
4. **Positivity:** Growth-oriented language

---

**Remember:** Parents should easily understand the message regardless of their own education level. Keep it clear, specific, and accessible.
